# Indicator 116 PIT_REPORT — 2026-11-12 KST

**Verdict: BLOCKED.** Historical first-release *dates* exist for both DGS2 and DGS10, but the existing ALFRED/FRED `realtime_start` field is date-granular and does not establish the actual time the values became accessible to this research system. The [FRED real-time-period documentation](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/fred/realtime_period.html) describes historical known-as-of dates; [vintage dates](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/fred/series_vintagedates.html) identify days when values were released or revised. Neither field is a row-level intraday publication timestamp.

The Federal Reserve [H.15 release page](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h15/) says the release is posted on business days at **4:15 p.m. U.S. Eastern time**, with holiday and closure exceptions; the [January 2020 release calendar](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/2020-january.htm) lists the scheduled dates and time. Treasury's underlying quotes are obtained near 3:30 p.m. Eastern, which is an input measurement time, not the public posting time. The current [DGS2](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS2) and [DGS10](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS10) pages expose a recent `Updated` timestamp, but that cannot establish every historical day's FRED ingestion time.

For illustration, a scheduled 2020-01-06 16:15 EST release is **2020-01-07 06:15 KST**; during U.S. daylight time, 2020-07-06 16:15 EDT is **2020-07-07 05:15 KST**. A Korean 09:00 decision on the U.S. *observation* date is therefore unsafe. The FRED first-release sample gives the 2020-01-06 observation a `realtime_start` of 2020-01-07; applying the H.15 scheduled clock would put it at 2020-01-08 06:15 KST, before a hypothetical 09:00 decision that day. This is a **conditional timing calculation**, not proof of actual posting or Korean exchange availability. Korean holidays and any provider delay require row-by-row handling.

The 2010–2026 annual audit found two same-day observation/vintage records (2015-04-01 and 2015-04-02) in both series, whereas most records have later vintage dates. These two records require direct historical release verification; a blanket one-day lag rule would be incorrect. Missing observations stay missing, and the 2013-01-31 DGS2-only gap prevents that day's spread calculation.

The existing Regime190 `pit_eligibility()` returned `BLOCKED_PIT` with **zero** eligible indicator IDs. The tracked indicator-116 inventory still marks `backtest_eligible=NO` and publication and decision-time availability unverified. The read-only operating DB probe returned `SCHEMA_VERIFIED_READ_ONLY_BOUNDED`, but 20/20 recent `market_rates_snapshot` sample rows lacked `released_at` and `vintage_date`. Its `available_at` column cannot substitute for primary publication evidence. No operating row or schema was changed.

Independent data validation covered 21 Treasury/FRED dates. It did **not** prove full historical revision equivalence, actual FRED availability time, or a sealed Korean-market target/holdout. A candidate chronological period exists in the raw data, but no untouched, PIT-approved holdout was established. Thus **PIT_VERIFIED=FALSE** and official use is blocked. No date or missing value was filled by an assumed release time.
